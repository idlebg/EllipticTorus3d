How the torus works

Both coordinates of a point on y² = x³ + ax + b live in Fp = {0, …, p−1} and wrap around at p, so the plane Fp × Fp is already a torus. The view wraps x once around the hole (u = 2π·x/p) and y once around the tube (v = 2π·y/p). The flat map is the same square with opposite edges glued.

Negation keeps x and sends y to p − y, so −P is the mirror image of P through the equatorial plane. P and −P always share a meridian ring. The point at infinity 𝒪 has no coordinates and is never drawn.

The chord through P₁ and P₂ is the line y ≡ λx + c (mod p). On the torus that line winds once around the hole and λ times around the tube (λ taken in (−p/2, p/2]); it meets the curve a third time at R = −(P₁ + P₂), and reflecting R gives the sum. For 256-bit curves the winding is astronomically large, so only the three points and the mirror step are drawn.

With a = 0 and p ≡ 1 (mod 3) there is a cube root of unity β, and (x, y) ↦ (βx, y) maps the curve to itself. On secp256k1 that is multiplication by λ, the GLV endomorphism; the three points sharing a y lie on one parallel ring.

Step (N) puts the result into P₁, so the operation runs again from there: with P₁ = P₂ = G it walks G, 2G, 3G, … around the surface, and with 2·P₁ it walks the doublings. Scalars stay readable (5G) whenever the page knows them.

Shapes

The Shape section (S) wraps the same points onto other surfaces, and the surface morphs from one to the next. The lemniscate and trefoil tubes are still tori, bent into a figure-eight or tied into a (2, 3) knot, so everything carries over. A knot is chiral: no mirror maps it onto itself, so on the trefoil −P is P reflected inside its own ring (across the plane of the knot's direction and the torus normal there) rather than through one plane. The cylinder, sphere, ellipsoid, icosahedron and wavy plane cut the torus open at y ≈ p/2: y is centred, so y = 0 lies on the mirror plane and P and −P stay mirror images, but rings and the chord break where they cross the cut. The icosahedron carries the sphere map pushed out radially onto its faces. The Klein bottle closes up in y but glues its x seam with a twist, y ↦ p/2 − y, so lines break there; it has no outside and is drawn from both sides. On the wavy plane negation mirrors through the upright plane along its middle.
