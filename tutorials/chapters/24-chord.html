24 Adding points: the chord

Level 2 · Learner. You will learn how two points of a curve are added, do one addition by hand, and see why the line through them winds around the donut. Read first: 21 Clock arithmetic and 23 Negation.

The chord rule

To add two points P₁ and P₂ of an elliptic curve:

  1. draw the line through them, the chord;
  2. it meets the curve in exactly one more point, R;
  3. the sum is the mirror image of R: P₁ + P₂ = −R.

Why exactly one more point? Put the line y = λx + c into the curve’s equation. You get an equation of degree 3 in x, and a cubic has three roots. Two of them are x₁ and x₂, the points you started with, so the third one is forced. For a cubic x³ − λ²x² + …, the three roots add up to λ², which gives the formula

x₃ = λ² − x₁ − x₂, then R = (x₃, λx₃ + c), and P₁ + P₂ = (x₃, −(λx₃ + c)).

Here λ (“lambda”) is the slope, λ = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁), with the division done mod p (multiply by the inverse).

Example 24.1 · G + 8G = 9G: the chord winds three times

The preset “y² = x³ + 7 over F₂₂₃, Programming Bitcoin” opens with exactly this addition: P₁ = G = (47, 71) and P₂ = 8G = (116, 55).

Open this view: G + 8G on F₂₂₃

Do it yourself

  1. In Curve, pick the preset y² = x³ + 7 over F₂₂₃, Programming Bitcoin.
  2. In the 3D view, click the result to pin its card.
  3. In the 3D view, hover R; its card stays until you hover another point.
  4. Read the Relations section in the panel.

Look at:

  • the white line through G and 8G, winding around the tube three times while it goes once around the hole;
  • the dashed ring R = (69, 137), where the line meets the curve again, and the short dashed mirror step from R to the green result (69, 86), whose card says k = 9;
  • Relations: slope λ = 3, winding “λ ≡ 3 (mod p): the line wraps 3 times around the tube”, third point R = (69, 137), and P₁ + P₂ = (69, 86).

Why: by hand, mod 223:

  • λ = (55 − 71) / (116 − 47) = −16 / 69. Is it 3? Check: 3 × 69 = 207, and 207 ≡ −16 because 207 + 16 = 223. Yes, λ = 3.
  • x₃ = 3² − 47 − 116 = −154, and −154 + 223 = 69.
  • the sum’s y: 3 × (47 − 69) − 71 = −137, which is 86 on the clock. So G + 8G = (69, 86), and R = (69, 137) is its mirror image.

The line itself is y ≡ 3x + 153 (mod 223), so c = 153. As x runs once around (0 to 223), 3x runs three times as far, so the line wraps three times around the tube: that is the winding the legend reports.

Example 24.2 · On the flat map: three parallel segments

Open this view: G + 8G, flat map enlarged

Do it yourself

  1. In Curve, pick the preset y² = x³ + 7 over F₂₂₃, Programming Bitcoin.
  2. Click ⤢ on the flat map to enlarge it.

Look at: the enlarged flat map. The line is drawn as three parallel segments: each time it leaves the top edge it comes back at the bottom, because y wraps at 223.

Why: a line on the torus is a straight line on the flat square that wraps around its edges. Slope 3 means three crossings of the y range per crossing of the x range.

A word on λ: on the clock, 3, 226 and −220 are the same number, and they would give the same points of the line but different drawings between them. The visualizer always takes the value between −p/2 and p/2, closest to 0, so the line winds as few times as possible.

Special slopes

Example 24.3 · Slope 0: the chord is a ring around the hole

(92, 47) and (20, 47) have the same y.

Open this view: (92, 47) + (20, 47)

Do it yourself

  1. In Curve, pick the preset y² = x³ + 7 over F₂₂₃, Programming Bitcoin.
  2. Type (92, 47) in P₁ and (20, 47) in P₂.
  3. In the 3D view, click the result to pin its card.
  4. Read the Relations section in the panel.

Look at: the chord is the ring around the hole at y = 47 (the legend: “horizontal: the parallel ring through P₁”), and Relations says “λ ≡ 0 (mod p): the line wraps 0 times”. The third point on that ring is R = (111, 47), and the sum is its mirror image, (111, 176).

Why: λ = (47 − 47) / (20 − 92) = 0, so the line is y = 47 and never leaves its ring. Three points of F₂₂₃ have y = 47, and 35 The GLV endomorphism explains why points sharing a y come in threes on this curve.

When the line is too steep

Most chords on most curves are steep: λ is a random number on the clock, so its centred value is often in the hundreds. The visualizer draws the continuous line only while it winds at most 24 times; beyond that the flat map shows the line’s p separate points instead (for p up to 4000), and on Bitcoin’s 256-bit curve only R and the mirror step are drawn. 26 Subtraction shows a steep example, and 41 The mapping the details.

Try it yourself

  • Add G and 2G on F₂₂₃ (type G and 2G). What slope does Relations report, and is the line drawn?
  • Swap P₁ and P₂ with Swap: the sum stays the same, but which point is R?
  • Compute 8G + 9G by hand with the formula and check it in the visualizer.

Recap

  • P₁ + P₂ = −R, where R is the third point of the curve on the line through P₁ and P₂.
  • λ = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁) mod p, x₃ = λ² − x₁ − x₂; the line is y ≡ λx + c.
  • On the torus the line winds |λ| times around the tube (λ taken closest to 0); Relations and the legend report it.
