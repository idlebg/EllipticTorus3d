94 Glossary

The words these tutorials use, in alphabetical order, each with the chapter that explains it best. Names in bold are labels the visualizer itself shows.

Automorphism

A map from a curve to itself that respects the addition of points. Every curve has P ↦ ±P; curves with a = 0 and p ≡ 1 (mod 3) have six, (x, y) ↦ (βⁱx, ±y). See 43.

Baby-step giant-step

The discrete-log method the visualizer uses on curves with p up to 2²²: store about √n multiples of G, then step from the target point in jumps of √n·G until one matches. See 29.

β (beta) and λ (lambda)

β is a cube root of unity mod p (β³ = 1, β ≠ 1). On a curve with a = 0, (x, y) ↦ (βx, y) is an automorphism; on secp256k1 it equals multiplication by λ, a cube root of unity mod n. The β-orbit switch (key B) draws (βx, y) and (β²x, y). See 35.

b′ (b prime)

For a point (x, y) that is not on the curve, the b′ with y² = x³ + ax + b′: the curve the point really lies on. The visualizer names it in the message and the Inspector. See 21 and 36.

BIP32, BIP340, BIP341

Bitcoin improvement proposals: hierarchical deterministic keys (child keys from a parent key and a chain code), Schnorr signatures with x-only keys, and Taproot outputs (the tweak Q = P + tG). See 37.

Chord and tangent

The line through two points of a curve (chord), or the line touching it at one point (tangent). It meets the curve in one more point R, and the sum is −R. The Chord construction switch (key K) draws it. See 24 and 25.

Cofactor

E divided by the order of G: how many times larger the whole group is than the part G generates. 1 on secp256k1 and

P-256, 12 on F₂₂₃. See 28.

Compressed key

A public key written as 02 or 03 followed by x: 33 bytes on secp256k1. The prefix says whether y is even (02) or odd (03). See 33 and 34.

Cusp and node

The two kinds of singular cubic: y² = x³ has a cusp at (0, 0), y² = x³ − 3x + 2 a node at (1, 0). The visualizer flags them in the Δ row. See 46.

Discrete logarithm

For points G and Q = kG, the number k. Easy to find on toy curves, infeasible on secp256k1: the basis of Bitcoin’s security. See 29 and 14.

Discriminant (Δ)

4a³ + 27b² (mod p). If it is 0 the cubic has a repeated root and the curve is singular, not an elliptic curve. See 46.

Elliptic curve

Here: the points (x, y) with y² = x³ + ax + b (mod p), together with the point at infinity, for a prime p and a nonzero discriminant. Its points can be added, which makes them a group. See 21.

Finite field

A finite set of numbers in which you can add, subtract, multiply and divide (except by 0). The numbers 0 to p − 1 with arithmetic mod a prime p form the field F_p. See 21.

Flat map

The panel in the corner of the view (“Flat torus”): the square [0, p) × [0, p) with x across and y up, the torus cut open. ⤢ enlarges it. See 13.

Generator G

The fixed starting point of a curve’s public keys: a public key is kG. Every preset has one; the Generator G field holds it. See 14 and 32.

Guide rings

The two rings through a point on the torus: around the tube (fixed x, a meridian) and around the hole (fixed y, a parallel). See 13.

Hasse bound

For any elliptic curve over F_p, the trace t = p + 1 − #E satisfies |t| ≤ 2√p. The trace row checks it. See 22.

Hybrid key

A public key written as 06 or 07, x and y: the prefix repeats y’s parity. The visualizer flags a wrong parity bit. See 33.

Invalid-curve attack

Sending a victim a point that is not on its curve but on another one with a small subgroup, so that the victim’s secret leaks modulo the small order. Prevented by checking every received point. See 36 and 45.

j-invariant

A number that classifies curves up to change of coordinates: j = 1728 · 4a³ / (4a³ + 27b²). j = 0 for every curve with a = 0, including secp256k1. See 43.

Longitude and latitude

The angle around the vertical axis (longitude) and the angle north or south of the equator (latitude). On the sphere x sets the longitude and y the latitude. See 14.

Mod p

Arithmetic in which only the remainder after division by p is kept: numbers on a clock with p hours. See 21.

Negation

The mirror image of a point: −(x, y) = (x, p − y). P + (−P) is the point at infinity. See 15 and 23.

Order

The order of a point P is the smallest n with nP = 𝒪. The order of a curve is its number of points, #E. See 28.

P-256

The NIST curve secp256r1, used in TLS and passkeys: a ≡ −3, prime order, no β. See 39.

Point at infinity

𝒪, the extra point of every elliptic curve, the zero of its addition: P + 𝒪 = P, and P + (−P) = 𝒪. It has no coordinates and is never drawn; type O to use it. See 12 and 23.

Private key

A number k from 1 to n − 1, kept secret. See 32.

Public key

The point kG of a private key k, published as a SEC or x-only encoding. See 32 and 33.

Quadratic twist

For a non-square d, the curve y² = x³ + ad²x + bd³. Every x is on the curve or, scaled by d, on its twist. See 44.

Relations, Inspector, Curve facts

Three sections of the panel: how P₁ and P₂ relate, every detail of the last point hovered, and what the visualizer works out about the curve. See 11.

secp256k1

Bitcoin’s curve: y² = x³ + 7 over the prime p = 2²⁵⁶ − 2³² − 977, with prime order n. See 31.

SEC encoding

The standard ways to write a point as bytes: compressed (02/03), uncompressed (04), hybrid (06/07). See 33.

Step

The Step ▸ button (key N): puts the result into P₁ so the operation runs again. With P₂ = G it walks the multiples of G. See 27.

Subgroup

A part of a group that is a group by itself, such as the multiples of G, written ⟨G⟩. See 28.

Supersingular

A curve whose trace is divisible by p; for p > 3 that means #E = p + 1. y² = x³ + b is supersingular when p ≡ 2 (mod 3). See 43.

Torsion

The points of a given order or its divisors: the 2-torsion points are those with 2P = 𝒪 (y = 0), the 7-torsion those with 7P = 𝒪. “Full” ℓ-torsion means all ℓ² of them are on the curve. See 23 and 42.

Torus

The surface of a donut. Points of a curve live on it because both coordinates wrap around at p. See 13.

Trace

t = p + 1 − #E: how far the number of points is from p + 1. See 22.

x-only key

A public key written as its x coordinate alone (32 bytes on secp256k1), meaning the point with even y. Used by BIP340 and Taproot. See 34.
