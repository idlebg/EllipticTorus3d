Level 3 · Bitcoiner · chapter 31

31 secp256k1 in the panel

Level 3 · Bitcoiner. Bitcoin’s curve as the visualizer shows it: its parameters, what each row of Curve facts says, and why two remainders of p matter for key handling. Read first: 11 Start here and 15 Mirror images; Level 2 for the arithmetic.

The parameters

secp256k1 is y² = x³ + 7 over F_p (a = 0, b = 7) with

  • p = 2²⁵⁶ − 2³² − 977 = fffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffefffffc2f (decimal 115792089237316195423570985008687907853269984665640564039457584007908834671663);
  • G = (x, y) with x = 79be667ef9dcbbac55a06295ce870b07029bfcdb2dce28d959f2815b16f81798 and y = 483ada7726a3c4655da4fbfc0e1108a8fd17b448a68554199c47d08ffb10d4b8;
  • n, the order of G, = fffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffebaaedce6af48a03bbfd25e8cd0364141 (decimal 115792089237316195423570985008687907852837564279074904382605163141518161494337), a prime;
  • h = 1: #E = n, so every point except 𝒪 is a multiple of G and has order n.

The trace t = p + 1 − n is 432420386565659656852420866390673177327, a 129-bit number well inside the Hasse bound |t| ≤ 2√p. The visualizer recognises the curve from (p, a, b), whichever way p is typed, and then uses the known n, h, β and λ instead of counting points.

Example 31.1 · The Curve facts of secp256k1

Open this view: secp256k1’s Curve facts

Do it yourself

  1. In Curve, pick the preset secp256k1: G and −G (mirror pair).
  2. Read the Curve section in the panel.

Look at each row:

  • p: shortened to its ends, with the full value in the tooltip and behind the copy button, then “256-bit prime (Miller–Rabin)”: primality is tested with 13 fixed bases, which is deterministic only below 3.3 × 10²⁴, hence the label;
  • a, b: 0, 7;
  • p mod: p ≡ 3 (mod 4), p ≡ 1 (mod 3) (see below);
  • named: secp256k1, Δ: non-singular, j: 0 (a = 0);
  • n and h: the subgroup order (full value in the tooltip) and “1, so every point ≠ 𝒪 has order n”;
  • β: a nontrivial cube root of unity mod p and the automorphism (x, y) ↦ (βx, y) (35 GLV).

Why p mod 4 matters. Decompressing a 02/03 key needs a square root mod p. For p ≡ 3 (mod 4) it is one exponentiation: y = (x³ + 7)^((p+1)/4) mod p, then pick y or p − y by the prefix’s parity. For G: x³ + 7 ≡ 4866d6a5ab41ab2c6bcc57ccd3735da5f16f80a548e5e20a44e4e9b8118c26f2, and raising it to (p + 1)/4 = 3fffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffbfffff0c gives 483ada7726a3c4655da4fbfc0e1108a8fd17b448a68554199c47d08ffb10d4b8, which is G’s y. Primes ≡ 1 (mod 4) need the slower Tonelli–Shanks loop; the visualizer has both.

Why p mod 3 matters. p ≡ 1 (mod 3) means F_p contains the cube roots of unity, so with a = 0 the map (x, y) ↦ (βx, y) is an automorphism of the curve. On the group it acts as multiplication by λ = 5363ad4cc05c30e0a5261c028812645a122e22ea20816678df02967c1b23bd72 (mod n), with β = 7ae96a2b657c07106e64479eac3434e99cf0497512f58995c1396c28719501ee. That is the GLV endomorphism libsecp256k1 uses to speed up multiplication.

Example 31.2 · G in full, in the Inspector

Open this view: G in the Inspector

Do it yourself

  1. In Curve, pick the preset secp256k1: G and −G (mirror pair).
  2. In the 3D view, click P₁ to pin its card.
  3. Read the Inspector section in the panel.

Look at: x and y as full 32-byte hex with copy buttons, “even, prefix 02 / 06”, k = 1, order “n (prime)”, and every SEC encoding. The cards shorten x and y to their first ten and last six hex digits; the Inspector never shortens.

Example 31.3 · p typed three ways

Open this view: p = 2**256 − 0x1000003d1

p typed as Curve facts open
2^256 - 2^32 - 977 named secp256k1 open
2**256 - 0x1000003d1 named secp256k1 open
0xfffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffefffffc2f named secp256k1 open

Do it yourself

  1. In Curve, pick the preset secp256k1: G and −G (mirror pair).
  2. Type 2**256 - 0x1000003d1 in Prime p.
  3. Read the Curve section in the panel.

Look at: the named row stays “secp256k1” for every spelling in the table. Fields accept integer expressions (+ - * / % ^ **, brackets, hex 0x, binary 0b, octal 0o, _ separators); ^ is a power, not XOR. The evaluated p is what the facts show and what the copy button copies.

Try it yourself

  • Change b to 5: the curve is no longer named, n and β disappear from the facts, and #E is “not counted (p above 2²²)”.
  • Type (n-1)G into P₁ and read P₁ vs G in Relations (32 explains it).
  • Hover the n row of the facts to see its full value in the tooltip.

Recap

  • secp256k1: a = 0, b = 7, p = 2²⁵⁶ − 2³² − 977, prime order n, cofactor 1.
  • p ≡ 3 (mod 4) makes decompression one exponentiation; p ≡ 1 (mod 3) gives β and the GLV endomorphism.
  • The panel shortens long values but keeps them whole in tooltips, copy buttons and the Inspector.
