Level 2 · Learner · chapter 21

21 Clock arithmetic and the curve equation

Level 2 · Learner. You will learn arithmetic “mod p”, check by hand that a point lies on a curve, and see what the visualizer does with a point that does not, or with a coordinate that is too big. Read first: 12 A curve is a cloud of dots and 13 Why a donut.

Arithmetic mod p

Pick a whole number p, here p = 223. Arithmetic mod p (“modulo p”) is ordinary arithmetic followed by one more step: divide by p and keep only the remainder. The remainder is always one of 0, 1, …, p − 1, the p hours of a clock with p hours.

  • 220 + 5 = 225, and 225 = 1 × 223 + 2, so 220 + 5 ≡ 2 (mod 223). Read “≡” as “is the same as, on this clock”.
  • 270 = 1 × 223 + 47, so 270 ≡ 47: going 270 hours forward lands on hour 47.
  • −152 + 223 = 71, so −152 ≡ 71: a negative number is counted backwards from 0.

Adding, subtracting and multiplying all work this way. When p is a prime number, dividing works too (every number except 0 has an inverse), and the numbers 0 to p − 1 with this arithmetic form a finite field, written F_p. F₂₂₃ is the one with 223 elements.

The curve equation

An elliptic curve here is given by three numbers a, b and p, and its points are the pairs (x, y) with

y² ≡ x³ + ax + b (mod p),

together with one extra point, 𝒪, the point at infinity. The Curve section shows a, b and p, and the facts the visualizer works out about them.

Example 21.1 · G = (47, 71) is on y² = x³ + 7 over F₂₂₃

Open this view: (47, 71) on F₂₂₃

Do it yourself

  1. In Curve, pick the preset y² = x³ + 7 over F₂₂₃, Programming Bitcoin.
  2. Empty P₂.
  3. In Display, turn off Chord construction (K).
  4. In the 3D view, click P₁ to pin its card.

Look at: the message under P₁, “(x, y) pair, on the curve”, and the Curve facts: p = 223, an 8-bit prime; a, b = 0, 7; Δ “4a³ + 27b² ≢ 0, non-singular”; #E = 252.

Why: check it by hand. Here a = 0 and b = 7, so the right-hand side is x³ + 7:

  • right: 47³ = 103823, and 103823 + 7 = 103830 = 465 × 223 + 135, so x³ + 7 ≡ 135;
  • left: 71² = 5041 = 22 × 223 + 135, so y² ≡ 135.

Both sides are 135 on the 223-hour clock, so (47, 71) is a point of the curve. The visualizer does the same test for every point you type, with numbers of any size.

The other facts, briefly: p mod says p ≡ 3 (mod 4) and p ≡ 1 (mod 3), two remainders that matter later (31, 35); Δ is the discriminant, which must not be 0 or the curve has a sharp point (46); j is the j-invariant, 0 for every curve with a = 0 (43); #E and trace are the subject of 22 Counting points.

Example 21.2 · A point that is not on the curve

Change the 71 to 72.

Open this view: (47, 72) on F₂₂₃

Do it yourself

  1. In Curve, pick the preset y² = x³ + 7 over F₂₂₃, Programming Bitcoin.
  2. Type (47, 72) in P₁.
  3. Empty P₂.
  4. In Display, turn off Chord construction (K).
  5. In the 3D view, click P₁ to pin its card.
  6. Read the Points section in the panel.

Look at:

  • the message under P₁, in red: “(x, y) pair, NOT on the curve: b′ = 150”;
  • the point itself, drawn with a dashed outline;
  • Relations: “not on this curve; it satisfies y² = x³ + ax + b′ with b′ = 150 (invalid-curve point)”;
  • the Inspector: a row b′ = 150, “this point lives on y² = x³ + ax + b′”.

Why: 72² = 5184, and 5184 − 47³ = −98639 ≡ 150 (mod 223). So (47, 72) does satisfy an equation of the same shape, just with b′ = 150 instead of 7: it is a point of a different curve. The visualizer still draws it (that is useful), but it refuses to add it to anything until you say so. 36 Invalid points explains why that refusal matters for Bitcoin.

Example 21.3 · Coordinates are read mod p

Open this view: (270, 71) on F₂₂₃

typed in P₁ the message under P₁ the point open
(47, 71) (x, y) pair, on the curve (47, 71) open
(270, 71) (x, y) pair, on the curve, coordinates reduced mod p (47, 71) open
(47, -152) (x, y) pair, on the curve, coordinates reduced mod p (47, 71) open
(0x2f, 0x47) (x, y) pair, on the curve (47, 71) open
(2*23 + 1, 71) (x, y) pair, on the curve (47, 71) open
(47, 72) (x, y) pair, NOT on the curve: b′ = 150 (47, 72) open

Do it yourself

  1. In Curve, pick the preset y² = x³ + 7 over F₂₂₃, Programming Bitcoin.
  2. Type (270, 71) in P₁.
  3. Empty P₂.
  4. In Display, turn off Chord construction (K).
  5. Read the Points section in the panel.

Look at: the table. Every field takes numbers in decimal or hex (0x2f), and whole expressions such as 2*23 + 1. A coordinate outside 0 to 222 is wrapped onto the clock, and the message then ends with “coordinates reduced mod p”, so it never happens silently.

Why: 270 − 223 = 47 and −152 + 223 = 71, so (270, 71) and (47, -152) are the same point as (47, 71). The visualizer reads every input on the clock, because that is the only place the point lives.

Try it yourself

  • Check (116, 55) by hand: is 55² ≡ 116³ + 7 (mod 223)? Type it into P₁ to confirm.
  • Type (47, 71 + 223) into P₁. What does the message say?
  • Change b from 7 to 8 in Curve: G = (47, 71) is no longer on the curve, and the message under Generator G turns red. Which b′ does it name?

Recap

  • Mod p means “keep the remainder after dividing by p”; with p prime, the numbers 0 to p − 1 form the field F_p.
  • A point is on the curve when y² ≡ x³ + ax + b (mod p); the visualizer checks every input.
  • An off-curve point is drawn dashed and named with the b′ of the curve it really lies on; coordinates are reduced mod p, with a note.
