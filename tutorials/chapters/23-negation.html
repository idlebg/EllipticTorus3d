Level 2 · Learner · chapter 23

23 Negation

Level 2 · Learner. You will learn the rule −P = (x, p − y) as arithmetic, why a point plus its negation is the point at infinity 𝒪, and which points are their own negation. Read first: 15 Mirror images and 21 Clock arithmetic.

The rule

Chapter 15 showed the picture: every point has a mirror image. In numbers: if (x, y) satisfies y² ≡ x³ + ax + b, so does (x, −y), because (−y)² = y². On the clock, −y is p − y (and −0 is 0). So the negation of P = (x, y) is

−P = (x, p − y).

The points of an elliptic curve form a group: there is an addition (chapters 24 and 25), 𝒪 plays the role of zero (P + 𝒪 = P), and every point has a negative with P + (−P) = 𝒪. The visualizer shows all three facts.

Example 23.1 · A point plus its mirror image is 𝒪

On the small curve F₁₇: (7, 6) and (7, 11), since 17 − 6 = 11.

Open this view: (7, 6) and (7, 11) on F₁₇

Do it yourself

  1. In Curve, pick the preset y² = x³ + 2x + 2 over F₁₇, Paar & Pelzl.
  2. Type (7, 6) in P₁ and (7, 11) in P₂.
  3. Click ⤢ on the flat map to enlarge it.
  4. In the 3D view, click P₁ and P₂ to pin their cards.

Look at:

  • the cards: (7, 6) is 9G and (7, 11) is 10G, and 9 + 10 = 19, the number of points of this curve;
  • Relations: “P₂ = −P₁: yes”, and P₁ + P₂ is “𝒪, the point at infinity”;
  • the chord row: “vertical line x = x₁: it meets the curve only at P, −P and 𝒪”;
  • the enlarged flat map: all 18 dots come in pairs, one above the dashed middle line and one below, on the same vertical line.

Why: adding means drawing a line through the two points (24 Adding points). Through P and −P that line is vertical (both have the same x), and a vertical line meets the curve in no third point. The group law says the answer is then 𝒪, which is why 𝒪 is called the point at infinity: it is where all vertical lines meet. On the donut that line is the ring around the tube through both points.

Points that are their own mirror image

If y = 0, then p − y = p ≡ 0, so −P = P: the point is its own negation, and P + P = 𝒪. These points are where x³ + ax + b = 0. A cubic has at most three roots, so a curve has 0, 1 or 3 such points. They sit exactly on the mirror plane: on the donut, on its outer rim (y = 0 means v = 0°).

Example 23.2 · The three points with y = 0

F₂₂₃ has three of them: (6, 0), (11, 0) and (206, 0).

Open this view: (6, 0) + (11, 0) on F₂₂₃

Do it yourself

  1. In Curve, pick the preset y² = x³ + 7 over F₂₂₃, Programming Bitcoin.
  2. Type (6, 0) in P₁ and (11, 0) in P₂.
  3. In Display, turn off Chord construction (K).
  4. Press 2 for the top view.
  5. In the 3D view, click P₁, P₂ and the result to pin their cards.
  6. Read the Relations section in the panel.

Look at:

  • from the top, all three points on the outer rim of the donut;
  • Relations: (6, 0) + (11, 0) = (206, 0), the third one; 2·P₁ and 2·P₂ are both 𝒪;
  • each card’s order: 2.

Why: the three points with y = 0, together with 𝒪, form a small group of their own, in which every element is its own negative and the sum of any two is the third. Their order is 2: one addition of the point to itself already gives 𝒪. A mathematician calls them the 2-torsion points.

Example 23.3 · A point that is its own mirror image

Type (6, 0) into P₁ and click the mirror button next to P₂: it writes (6, 0) again.

Open this view: (6, 0) and its mirror image

Do it yourself

  1. In Curve, pick the preset y² = x³ + 7 over F₂₂₃, Programming Bitcoin.
  2. Type (6, 0) in P₁.
  3. Click the mirror button next to P₂ (its tooltip: “Set P₂ = −P₁, the mirror image”).
  4. Press 3 for the side view.
  5. In the 3D view, click P₁ to pin its card.
  6. Read the Relations section in the panel.

Look at: Relations says “P₂ = P₁: yes”, the sum is 𝒪, and the chord is the vertical ring through the point. Its row P₂ = −P₁ says “no”: for a point with y = 0 the visualizer reports the pair as equal rather than as mirror images, although here they are both.

Why: the mirror button computes −P₁ = (6, 223 − 0) = (6, 0), the same point. Adding a point to itself uses the tangent line (25 Doubling); at a point with y = 0 the tangent is vertical, so the result is 𝒪 again.

Try it yourself

  • On F₂₂₃, type 5G into P₁ and click the mirror button. Which k does P₂ get? (5 + k must be 21.)
  • Find the points with y = 0 on y² = x³ + 2x + 3 over F₉₇. How many are there? Check with 2·P₁.
  • Type O into P₂ (the letter O means 𝒪). What is P₁ + 𝒪?

Recap

  • −P = (x, p − y); P + (−P) = 𝒪, drawn as the vertical ring through both points.
  • Points with y = 0 are their own negation; there are 0, 1 or 3 of them, all on the mirror plane.
  • 𝒪 acts as zero: P + 𝒪 = P.
