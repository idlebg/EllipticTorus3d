Level 3 · Bitcoiner · chapter 39

39 P-256 next to secp256k1

Level 3 · Bitcoiner. NIST P-256 (secp256r1), the curve of TLS, passkeys and smart cards, side by side with Bitcoin’s: what is the same, what differs, and why it has no β. Read first: 31 secp256k1 in the panel.

The parameters

P-256 is y² = x³ + ax + b over F_p with

  • p = 2²⁵⁶ − 2²²⁴ + 2¹⁹² + 2⁹⁶ − 1 = ffffffff00000001000000000000000000000000ffffffffffffffffffffffff;
  • a = p − 3 = ffffffff00000001000000000000000000000000fffffffffffffffffffffffc, that is a ≡ −3;
  • b = 5ac635d8aa3a93e7b3ebbd55769886bc651d06b0cc53b0f63bce3c3e27d2604b;
  • n = ffffffff00000000ffffffffffffffffbce6faada7179e84f3b9cac2fc632551, prime, and h = 1;
  • G = 036b17d1f2e12c4247f8bce6e563a440f277037d812deb33a0f4a13945d898c296 (compressed).

Like secp256k1 it has prime order (cofactor 1): by Hasse’s bound the number of points lies within 2√p of p + 1, and the only multiple of n in that window is n itself. Like secp256k1, p ≡ 3 (mod 4), so decompression is one square root, and p ≡ 1 (mod 3). Unlike secp256k1, a ≠ 0.

Example 39.1 · The Curve facts of P-256

Open this view: P-256’s Curve facts

Do it yourself

  1. In Curve, pick the preset P-256: G and 2G.
  2. Read the Curve section in the panel.

Look at: the a row, “≡ −3”: the visualizer names a small negative representative when there is one; named “P-256 (secp256r1)”; j, the j-invariant 1198954424ebb0f8479de43131caece8ee0a9b13a558c21e0b2f74e3fcd36aa3, not 0; and no β row: the β-orbit switch is greyed out.

Why: the GLV map (x, y) ↦ (βx, y) needs a = 0 (so that x³ + b ignores the cube root of unity). With a = −3, βx does not give a point, even though p ≡ 1 (mod 3) provides the β. a = −3 was chosen instead because it makes the doubling formula in Jacobian coordinates cheaper. The two curves trade one speed-up for another.

Example 39.2 · G + 2G = 3G on P-256

Open this view: G + 2G on P-256

Do it yourself

  1. In Curve, pick the preset P-256: G and 2G.
  2. In the 3D view, click the result to pin its card.
  3. Read the Relations section in the panel.

Look at: the same construction as on secp256k1: only R and the mirror step are drawn, and the result’s card says k = 3. Relations gives 3G = 025ecbe4d1a6330a44c8f7ef951d4bf165e6c6b721efada985fb41661bc6e7fd6c; OpenSSL computes the same point (the build checks it).

Why: everything in Levels 2 and 3 that does not use a = 0 works on any curve: the chord and tangent, negation, k carried through, the key formats. 2G = 037cf27b188d034f7e8a52380304b51ac3c08969e277f21b35a60b48fc47669978.

Try it yourself

  • Switch the P-256 preset to the sphere and the trefoil: the shapes do not care which curve it is.
  • Type -G into P₂ and choose P₁ + P₂: 𝒪, as on every curve.
  • Change a from -3 to 0 with the same b and p: the curve is no longer named, and the β row appears (p ≡ 1 mod 3).

Recap

  • P-256: a ≡ −3, a random-looking b, prime order n, p ≡ 3 (mod 4) and p ≡ 1 (mod 3).
  • No GLV endomorphism of the β kind, because a ≠ 0; the visualizer greys out the β-orbit.
  • Every other construction carries over unchanged.
